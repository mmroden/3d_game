# Voice Test — register samples for redlining

Purpose: lock how each voice *sounds* before mass drafting. These lines are
disposable — mark up freely. Rules they follow: [script_guide.md](../script_guide.md).
Some presume open-question recommendations (Q2-B voiced Player, Q3-B late
counterfeit); redline those too if the presumption is wrong.

## THE HANDLER — "Maya" (working name)

Comms discipline eroding into a person. Always 2–3s lagged.

Run one, cold open (the tutorial-as-diagnostics):

> **[sample H1]** "—copy? Dawn Six-Actual, do you copy? Link shows green on
> my end, but your vitals just did something I have never seen."

> **[sample H2]** "Okay. If you're receiving: drift check. Pitch, yaw, roll.
> Nice and slow."

> **[sample H3]** "Take your time. Signal's running three seconds each way —
> nobody's rushing anybody."
> *(establishes the lag diegetically in the first minute; the tell depends on this)*

> **[sample H4]** "Your loadout reads armed. Anything moves out there and
> isn't tagged blue, it isn't ours."

Reacting to telemetry only (she cannot see what the player sees):

> **[sample H5]** "Your readings just spiked. Talk to me — what's in there?"

> **[sample H6]** "Say again your last? Because it sounded like you said
> *living room*."

Amnesia beat, early:

> **[sample H7]** *(a beat of dead air longer than the lag)* "…It's Maya,
> Dawn Six. It's still Maya."

Protocol erosion, late meta-tier:

> **[sample H8]** "Command wants me to read you the continuity script.
> To hell with the script. You come home, Dawn Six."

Counterfeit handler (Q3-B; same actor, Other's processing chain, answers

## THE OTHER

Precise English, wrong ontology. Grammar tier noted per line.

T1 — nominal/imperative, no pronouns, no articles (M1, heavily garbled):

> **[sample O1]** "Synchronization: initial. Further challenge required.
> Continue."

> **[sample O2]** "Baseline established. Continuing to develop contact.
> Iterating."
> *(the invariant line — locked verbatim, bible rule 3)*

T2 — third-person designation, present tense, no contractions:

> **[sample O3]** "The subject adapts. Adaptation is the signal. The rest
> is noise."

> **[sample O4]** "The subject grieves for furniture. This is noted.
> This is not understood."

T3 — second person arrives; measurement metaphor; still no past tense:

> **[sample O5]** "You adapt faster when afraid. Your fear has a shape.
> It is efficient."

> **[sample O6]** "This is the fastest channel you have. Words are slower.
> We will get to words."

> **[sample O7]** *(entering the house, first time)* "This place is load-bearing
> in you. Nothing here weighs anything. Explain the difference."

T4 — first person, past tense, contractions (ship frontier):

> **[sample O8]** "I. — That word cost more than you will ever know. I."
> *(SIGNATURE: the first "I")*

> **[sample O9]** "I had six hundred years of warnings to give. I've been
> rehearsing them for sixty-five million."

> **[sample O10]** *(the trajectory plot, ch4)* "You recognize the coastline.
> I have never been able to stop rendering it."

Death fragments (post-first-clear pool, T2–T3):

> **[sample O11]** "Death response: catalogued. Your species rehearses
> endings. Mine archived them."

> **[sample O12]** "You return. Good. Returning is the only mercy in the
> protocol."

## What to redline

1. **Handler:** how much warmth under the procedure? H7/H8 set the ceiling.
   Is "Maya" a keeper or does the handler stay callsign-only longer?
2. **The Other:** O4/O5 set the register — instrumentation-speak about
   emotion. Too clinical? Not clinical enough? Does the tier ladder read as
   an arc in these ten lines alone? (It should.)
3. **Player:** P1–P5 assume a dry, competent-but-rattled pilot. Gender/age
   deliberately unmarked so casting stays open. Right survivor archetype?
4. Any line you'd cut on the tenth hearing — say which and why; that
   calibrates my ear for the commons.
