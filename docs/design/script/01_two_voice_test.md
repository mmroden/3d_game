# Voice Test 2 — the two-voice cast (pilot corpus model)

Presumes cast model A (2026-07-18): TWO recorded voices, both performed by
Mark — the PILOT (deep bass, dry, low-energy) and the AI (same session,
clean takes; all garble is post-processing per script_guide.md). The
handler, if it survives, is text-only and does not appear here. The AI's
language corpus is the PILOT'S OWN SPEECH on the link: every early AI
line is a distorted reuse of words the pilot has actually said.

New convention this model adds: **`corpus:` tags.** An AI line at T1–T2
cites the pilot line ID(s) it learned its vocabulary from. The script
becomes an audit trail of the acquisition — nothing in the AI's mouth
before the pilot put it there. (T3+ loosens as the AI generalizes;
first UN-sourced word is a beat in itself.)

Disposable lines. Redline freely.

---

## THE PILOT — bass register

Talking into a laggy, then dead, link. Short declaratives sit better in
a low voice than questions do — questions go up, bass goes down. Written
for a delivery that never rises.

Run one, cold open:

> **[PLT-01]** "Link check. Somebody copy. …Copy means answer."

> **[PLT-02]** "Drift's wrong. Up is not staying up. Going slow."

> **[PLT-03]** "Talking so the recorder has something. That's all this is."

First contact with the wrongness:

> **[PLT-04]** "There's a chair out here. Whole living room. Noting it
> and moving on, because the alternative is thinking about it."

> **[PLT-05]** "I know this hallway. …Log that. I know this hallway."

Into the dead link (mid/late meta — the monologue habit is the corpus):

> **[PLT-06]** "Still transmitting. Habit now. If anyone's archiving this:
> the coffee up here is theoretical."

> **[PLT-07]** "Something's listening. Readings say so. Fine. Listen."

## THE AI — grammar tiers on the pilot's corpus

Precise English, wrong ontology (script_guide rules hold). Vocabulary at
T1–T2 is ONLY what the pilot has said. Garble survivability: load-bearing
words at both ends.

T1 — nominal/imperative; the pilot's own nouns handed back:

> **[AI-T1-01]** `corpus: PLT-01` "Copy. …Copy. Answer: pending. Continue."

> **[AI-T1-02]** `corpus: PLT-02` "Slow. Going-slow: acceptable. Drift:
> corrected. Continue."

T2 — third person, present tense; the pilot becomes "the subject":

> **[AI-T2-01]** `corpus: PLT-03` "The subject talks so the recorder has
> something. The recorder is not the listener. This is known here."

> **[AI-T2-02]** `corpus: PLT-04, PLT-05` "The subject knows the hallway.
> The hallway is load-bearing in the subject. This is noted."

T3 — second person; measurement metaphor; first un-sourced word:

> **[AI-T3-01]** "You go slow where it hurts and fast where it doesn't.
> Your map of hurt is accurate. *Accurate* is my word. The first one."

T4 — first person, past tense (SIGNATURE territory, drafted for arc-read
only):

> **[AI-T4-01]** "I learned to speak from a man narrating his own
> disappearance. Everything I say is in your voice. Including this."

## What to redline

1. **Bass fit:** every pilot line deliverable in a low, flat register
   without strain? Anything that wants pitch, cut or rewrite.
2. **The corpus tell:** is the echo (PLT words inside AI lines) legible
   without being cute? AI-T1-02 is the test case.
3. **AI-T4-01** sets the endpoint of the whole two-voice conceit — right
   destination?
4. **Handler ghost:** with model A, does the dead link carry run one on
   its own, or does the opening need a text-channel handler beat?
